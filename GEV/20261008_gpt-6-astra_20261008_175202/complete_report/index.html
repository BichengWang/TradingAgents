Practical consequences include:

  • Avoid selecting a stop simply because its dollar distance looks small.
  • Distinguish a structural invalidation level from a noise-sensitive stop.

  • Allow for gaps and slippage: a stop does not guarantee its stated execution price.

A general sizing framework is:

Position size = maximum planned dollar risk ÷ entry-to-stop distance, subject to capital, liquidity, and execution constraints.
