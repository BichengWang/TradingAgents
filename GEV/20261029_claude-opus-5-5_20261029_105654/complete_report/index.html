6. Where I expect the bear to push¶

"47x run-rate earnings is too expensive." - Those multiples are built on trailing figures, and the business is changing fast. - Q3 operating income alone ($1.39B) is roughly 28% of TTM EBITDA ($5.0B). Annualizing one quarter that's still ramping is a floor for a growing business, not a ceiling. - Forward EPS of $25.29 sits about 32% above Q3's $19.2 annualized run-rate. At a 38% incremental margin, that bar looks reachable. - This is a premium multiple on a premium growth rate. The stock has also already de-rated 23% while earnings tripled.

"The GAAP earnings are noisy." - I agree, and I wouldn't use the $38.09 TTM EPS. The Q1'26 net income of $4.7B on $179M of operating income was clearly a non-operating gain. - That is why I anchor on operating income, which tripled, and on Q2 and Q3 EPS. The bull case doesn't depend on the one-offs.

"Free cash flow is just customer advances." - That is plausible, since current liabilities rose about 52% this year. But customers prepaying for turbine slots is evidence of demand. - Some working-capital normalization is possible, but it would unwind against tripled operating income and a backlog near $200B. - The capex ramp (4.3% of revenue vs 2.2%) is investment in capacity for that book, not a sign of weakness.

"Beat-and-sell. Caterpillar fell 9–12% on a beat." - That is a real sentiment risk. But CAT was punished for its Q4 outlook, while GEV's backlog and orders are the opposite of a soft outlook. - Oracle's $225B FY2030 revenue guide on 10-28 shows AI-infrastructure demand is intact. - Polymarket puts a 2026 recession at 6% and an AI-bubble burst at 8%.

"Insiders are selling." - The last three disclosed sales total about $11.5M, at $850–$1,059, from officers at a $238B company. - The CEO exercised options and made no open-market sale. The newest record is five months old.

"No rate cuts (97% priced)." - That caps multiple expansion, and I'll grant it. But GEV doesn't need expansion. It needs earnings to grow into the multiple, and they are growing faster than the multiple is falling.
