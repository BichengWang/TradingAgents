Bull Researcher¶

Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for CARR (Carrier Global, $53.33)

I'll set the terms of the debate. The bear will point to a $2.6B quarterly loss, a broken chart and a hawkish Fed. I'll argue that the cash flow, revenue trend and entry price point the other way.

1. The loss is an accounting event, and cash flow shows it¶

Q3 net income was –$2,593M, with an operating loss of –$2,273M. Here is what else happened in the same quarter:

  • Operating cash flow was about $813M, against about $341M a year ago. That is more than double.
  • Q3 free cash flow was about $674M, against about $224M. Nine-month FCF is $1.47B, up about 21%.
  • Below-the-line items looked normal. The gap between operating and net income was about $320M, in line with Q2's $324M. That fits a non-deductible, non-cash charge such as an impairment.
  • Assets fell $3.8B while liabilities fell only $0.7B. That is the shape of a write-down, not of cash leaving the building.

I'm inferring the nature of the charge, because the tools don't identify it, and the 10-Q should confirm it. But a business that booked a real economic loss would not produce its best cash quarter of the year at the same time.

The "worse leverage" story is also mostly mechanical. The write-down cut equity from $13.5B to $10.4B and lifted debt/equity from roughly 0.88x to 1.14x. Debt didn't rise, and the charge didn't touch cash or EBITDA.

2. Revenue is accelerating¶

Year-on-year growth ran +2.4%, +3.9%, then +12.3% across Q1 to Q3. Q3 revenue of $6.26B was the highest in the six-quarter series. Nine-month revenue is up 6.2%, and Q2 operating margin recovered to 13.0% from Q1's 4.8%.

I can't say whether the Q3 jump was volume, price or acquisitions, because I have no segment data. But a business that is "depending on data center to remain in business" does not usually grow 12% while its stock falls 29%.

3. The entry point is attractive¶

  • Price: $53.33 is in the bottom ~12% of the 52-week range ($50.24–$76.76) and about 29% below the June peak close.
  • Valuation: the forward P/E is 16.8x and the PEG is about 1.0.
  • Cash returns: TTM FCF is $1.94B, a roughly 4.5% yield. The dividend (~1.8% yield) takes only about 40–45% of that.
  • Insider signal: CEO Gitlin bought $1.02M of stock in the open market at $52.62. The stock is within 1.3% of that price today. He is the only insider who has bought on the open market, and no executive has sold on the open market since.

Risk/reward: the nearest hard reference below is the 49.46–49.52 January and November low, about 7% down. Above, the 200-day SMA at $61.27 is about 15% up, and the 50-day at $55.58 is about 4% up. That is roughly 2:1 before any fundamental re-rating.

4. The macro backdrop is hawkish but not recessionary¶

The Fed is hiking, and I won't pretend otherwise. But look at why:

  • Q3 real GDP growth is about 3.4% annualized and accelerating.
  • Unemployment is 4.1%.
  • Polymarket puts recession odds at 4%.
  • The 10-year yield has fallen about 45bp from its October peak of 5.31%.

Rate hikes in a strong economy are a very different setup from rate hikes into a slowdown. Industrial replacement demand holds up in this kind of economy.

The breakdown below the range also coincided with a market-wide shock. The VIX jumped from 16.61 to 28.18 on 11/05, the day CARR closed at 53.21. With a beta of 1.33, part of this move is market beta, not a verdict on the company.
