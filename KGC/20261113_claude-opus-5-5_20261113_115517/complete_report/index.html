1. The market is paying a distressed price for a business that isn't distressed¶

The price has fallen about as far as it would if earnings had been cut in half. They haven't been.

2. The balance sheet removes the bankruptcy-style tail risk¶

  • Cash and debt: Cash is $2.64B against $739M of debt, so net cash is about $1.9B, roughly 8% of market cap.
  • Liquidity: The current ratio is 2.87 and debt/equity is 0.07x.
  • Deleveraging: About $500M of debt was retired in Q4'25. Interest income ($25.5M) now exceeds interest expense ($7.2M).
  • Conservative view: Even if I subtract the $692M tax payable from cash, net liquidity is still about $1.2B.

This company can fund its growth capex, pay its dividend and keep buying back stock without needing a better gold price or open capital markets.

3. Capital returns are working harder at these prices¶

  • Over the last four quarters, KGC returned about $955M in buybacks and $185M in dividends, roughly 4.7% of market cap.
  • The share count is down about 2.6% year on year, and Q3 alone shrank it 1.35%.

4. The Q3 slowdown is partly timing, not just deterioration¶
