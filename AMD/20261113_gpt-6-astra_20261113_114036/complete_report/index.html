4. AMD risk/reward deserves particular scrutiny¶

Using 524.15 solely as an illustrative entry reference:

  • Distance to 585.30: 61.15.
  • Distance down to the reported daily SuperTrend at 454.03: 70.12.

That produces less than 1:1 prospective reward/risk, before costs or slippage, if those two references are used as target and invalidation.

This is not a forecast. It demonstrates why combining a nearby recovery objective with a distant trend stop can produce unattractive trade geometry.

A general position-sizing framework is:

Shares = planned dollar risk ÷ entry-to-stop distance

For AMD, gaps, slippage, and execution costs can cause actual losses to exceed that planned amount. A specific execution decision still requires an entry, time horizon, and risk budget.

Overall conclusion: AMD retains constructive long-term features, but the primary weekly trend, weak directional persistence, and sharp latest reversal argue against treating the rebound as confirmed. The stronger evidence would come from sustained price acceptance through the recovery references, supported by momentum and volume—not from the size of a single bounce.
