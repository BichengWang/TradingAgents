Practical implications:

  • Tight stops can sit inside ordinary recent price noise.
  • Wide structural stops require smaller position sizes.
  • ATR includes gaps and is a historical volatility measure—not a forecast or guaranteed trading range.
  • Stop orders do not guarantee fills at their trigger prices.

A useful sizing framework is:

Position size = permitted monetary risk ÷ (entry-to-stop distance + execution allowance)

The invalidation level should be chosen from the trade’s structure first. Position size should then accommodate that distance, rather than forcing a tight stop to justify a larger position.
