Selected indicator: atr Why it fits: It translates the unusually wide trading ranges into a position-sizing constraint.

The verified ATR is 16.65, approximately 9.7% of the current close.

That is substantial daily movement relative to the share price. Consequently:

  • Small fixed-dollar stops can sit well inside ordinary volatility.
  • Wider structural stops require smaller positions.
  • A stop order does not cap losses reliably through gaps or poor execution.
  • Distant SuperTrend references should not substitute for a trade-specific invalidation level.

A practical sizing framework is:

[ \text{Shares}= \frac{\text{Maximum permitted dollar loss}} {|\text{Entry}-\text{Stop}|+\text{per-share execution allowance}} ]

For illustration—not as a recommended NBIS stop—a two-ATR distance is 33.30. A maximum planned loss of 500 would permit approximately 15 shares before fees, slippage, and gap risk.

Choose the structural invalidation first, then size the position. Do not select a stop solely to accommodate a desired number of shares.
