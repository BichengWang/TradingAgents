These are not a trade decision.

  1. Holding a long position: the main trend still supports holding. Use 546.10 as the near-term warning: a daily close below it means trimming or hedging. Use the weekly stop around 494 as the line where the bullish case is wrong. Inference, not tested: a 2-ATR stop (563.28 − 32.6 ≈ 530.7) lands just under the 50 SMA and the bottom of the 09-21 gap. Several levels line up there, which makes 530–536 a sensible point to define risk.
  2. Considering a new long: don't buy into a heavy-volume down day. Wait for one of two setups:
  3. Price tests the 536–555 zone on lighter volume with a bullish reversal candle, and OBV holds above about 265M. Stop under about 530.
  4. Price closes back above the 10 EMA (about 573) on rising volume.
  5. Inference: entering near 546 with a stop at 530 risks about 16 points (1 ATR). Measured against a retest of 589–596, that is roughly 2.7–3:1.
  6. Short-term traders looking for downside: the trigger would be a daily close below 546.10, ideally with a MACD bearish crossover. First targets are 536–541 (50 SMA and the gap), then 497–500 if 533 gives way. Since the weekly trend is up, keep shorts small and short-lived.
  7. Hypothetical, not tested: if 551.95 (the 09-28 low) breaks, the double-top pattern measures to roughly 507 (596.44 − 551.95 = 44.5). That is near the August–September base. Treat it as a scenario only.
  8. Position size: a normal day moves about 16.3 points (2.9%). With a 1% account-risk budget and a stop near 536 (about 4.8% away), the position would be about 20% of capital. Cut that if ATR keeps rising.
