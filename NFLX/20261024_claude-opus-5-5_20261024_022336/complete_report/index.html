1. The market has priced a broken business. The numbers show a decelerating but excellent one.¶

The stock closed at $65.85 on Oct 23, about 44% below its 52-week high of $116.73. Here is what happened to the business over the same period:

  • Q3'26 revenue was $12.87B, up 11.8% YoY. Netflix is growing double digits on a ~$50B base. The CFO says double-digit growth is still achievable into 2027.
  • Operating margin was 34.6%, a record. Gross margin was 52.8%, also a record. Q3 operating margin was 19.3% in 2022 and is 34.6% today. Over those four years, revenue rose 62% and operating income rose about 191%.

  • The share count is shrinking. Diluted shares fell about 2% in two quarters, from ~4.30B to ~4.21B.

Earnings grew while the price fell by almost half. At $116.73 the stock would trade at about 34x today's TTM EPS. At $65.85 it trades at 19.0x trailing and 17.5x forward, with a PEG of 1.09. The decline is a multiple compression story, not an earnings collapse story.

2. The valuation holds up even after I adjust the flattering numbers¶

I'll concede the first adjustment before the bear raises it. Q1'26 net income of $5.28B included a one-off of roughly $1.75B above the run-rate. The tools don't identify its source, so TTM EPS of $3.46 is inflated.

That isn't deep value, and I won't call it that. It is a 34% operating margin franchise priced near the risk-free rate, with growth and buybacks on top.

The tails are instructive: - Re-rating: 25x run-rate EPS is ~$83.50, close to the 200-day SMA ($82.27) and the Sept 2 close of $82.73.
