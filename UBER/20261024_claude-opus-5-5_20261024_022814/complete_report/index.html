Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for UBER: A Better Business at a Worse Price

I'll start with the fact the bear has to deal with. UBER is down about 33% from its $100.35 high, to $66.70, but the business got better over that stretch. First-half 2026 operating income was $3.81B against $2.68B a year earlier, up 42%. The market has cut the multiple, not the earnings power. That gap is the opportunity.

1. The profit engine is working¶

  • Operating margin has risen every year: –5.7% (FY22), 3.0%, 6.4%, 10.7% (FY25), and 13–15% in the last three quarters.
  • Incremental margins are strong: about 29% in Q2'26 and 42% in Q1'26. Revenue growth is turning into profit at a high rate.
  • Cash generation is large and capex-light: FY25 free cash flow was about $9.8B, 18.8% of revenue, on capex under 1% of revenue. Over the last four quarters, FCF was about $10.1B by my statement-derived math.

The bear will point to the PEG of 6.0 and say the 14.6x P/E is flattered. I agree on part of that. TTM net income includes a $6.6B Q3'25 windfall, and GAAP EPS is distorted by tax and investment items, so I won't lean on it.

I'll lean on cash instead. At a $136.2B market cap:

  • Conservative FCF (the vendor's $7.24B) gives a 5.3% yield.
  • Statement-derived FCF (about $10.1B) gives a 7.4% yield.

Either way, that is a high FCF yield for a platform growing revenue 12%+ and operating income 30–40%+. My rough EV/EBITDA is about 19x. That isn't deep value, but it is reasonable for a business converting revenue to profit at these incremental margins.

3. Insiders are buying after selling high¶
