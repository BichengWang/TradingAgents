Bull Researcher¶

Bull Analyst: My opening case for UBER: The bear is right to question the recent rebound—but the investment case does not depend on one good trading day or on robotaxis becoming profitable soon. It rests on a large marketplace that is still growing while converting more of its revenue into operating profit.

In Q2 2026, UBER grew revenue 12.2% year over year to $14.19 billion, while operating income rose 30.3% to $1.89 billion. Operating margin improved from 11.46% to 13.32%. Across the first half, revenue grew 13.3% and operating income 42.4%. That is the bull’s strongest evidence of scalability: profit growth is outpacing sales growth without requiring an autonomous-vehicle breakthrough. First-half operating cash flow of $5.21 billion gives the company resources to pursue growth, although cash generation alone does not settle questions about its spending.

The next opportunity is to put more transactions through UBER’s existing marketplace. The reported $2.3 billion ezCater agreement could extend Uber Eats into workplace catering; Sur La Table adds retail selection. Planned London rides with Pony.ai offer a different kind of upside: UBER could distribute autonomous rides through its app rather than having to invent the vehicles. I would not book earnings from any of these initiatives today. Their appeal is that each could broaden the platform’s uses if execution and transaction economics prove sound.

Here is where I would push back on the bear case. Yes, growth has slowed from 2025’s 18.3% revenue increase, and a 5.28% 10-year Treasury yield and higher gasoline prices are real headwinds. But slower growth is not stalled growth, and the latest reported operating-profit gains provide a stronger counterweight than retail excitement does. September unemployment at 4.2% and August retail sales up approximately 1.13% month over month also do not yet point to a collapse in demand.

Nor should bulls wave away the financial objections. UBER’s June current ratio was 0.84, cash had fallen, and its large investing outflow needs explanation. The two supplied trailing-free-cash-flow figures—$7.25 billion from the vendor and approximately $10.12 billion derived from the statements—do not reconcile, so I would not pitch a single definitive FCF multiple. Reported EPS is also less dependable than operating income because net income has been volatile.
