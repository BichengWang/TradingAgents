2. The price has detached from the business¶

The stock is about 59% below its October 2025 closing peak while growth accelerated. - The forward P/E is 11.5x and the PEG is 0.7. That forward EPS is probably non-GAAP, so it isn't like-for-like with trailing numbers. - Even on a conservative GAAP run rate ($1.56 × 4, about $6.24), the stock trades at about 20x for a ~30% grower with rising margins. - Multiple compression did the damage, not earnings. That is what a financing-fear drawdown looks like, and it is different from a broken business.

3. The spending is chasing demand¶

Capex was 147% of revenue last quarter, and I won't pretend that's small. But total assets are up 68% year over year while revenue is up 30% and still accelerating, so the build-out is showing up in revenue. - Demand evidence: TikTok's US spokesperson reportedly reaffirmed Oracle as its primary cloud infrastructure provider. StockTwits posters cite a $664B backlog and $11.4B of customer prepayments. Those figures aren't corroborated in my feed, so I'm flagging them as unverified. - Cash flow: the $23.1B of operating cash flow in the August quarter is unusually high. It is consistent with large prepayments, but I want the 10-Q to confirm that. - Free cash flow direction: it improved to about -$5.4B from -$11.5B in the February quarter, the best of the quarters I could derive since August 2025.

4. The balance sheet is being reinforced¶

Equity jumped $24B in one quarter, to $66.8B. The likely explanation is equity issuance, which I'll verify in the 10-Q. That means dilution, but it also means management didn't fund everything with debt, and cash is $36.4B with a 1.17 current ratio. The bear is worried about funding risk, and a company that raises equity into a drawdown is addressing it.

  • Investor Day, Oct 28: this is a chance for management to put numbers on backlog conversion, capex, funding and free cash flow, which are the open questions.
  • Macro: the 10-year fell from 5.31% to 4.96%, recession odds are only 8%, and the 96% "no cuts" pricing is already in the stock.
