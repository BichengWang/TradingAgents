Bull Analyst: # The Bull Case for ANET: A Pullback in a Compounder, Not a Broken Thesis

I'll open with the facts, since the tape is loud right now. ANET has dropped about 9.8% in three sessions, from $216.74 to $195.42. I'll explain why I think that's an opportunity, and then take on the bear arguments I expect.

1. The business is accelerating¶

  • Revenue growth is speeding up. YoY growth went from +27% (Q3'25) to +35% (Q1'26) to +37.7% in Q2'26, on a base of $3.04B in quarterly revenue. TTM revenue is $10.54B.
  • Profits are growing as fast as sales. Q2 net income was $1.21B (+36%) and EPS was $0.95 (+36%).
  • Operating margin is ~45%. Opex fell from 20.5% to 17.5% of revenue year over year, so the company is getting more efficient while it scales.
  • Cash conversion is exceptional. TTM free cash flow is $3.89B, a 37% margin and ~96% of net income, with capex around 1.5% of revenue. H1'26 operating cash flow was $2.78B, up 51% YoY and about 124% of H1 net income.
  • The balance sheet is a fortress. Equity is $14.8B, working capital is ~$13B, and the current ratio is ~3.0.

Companies growing 38% with 45% operating margins and near-total cash conversion are rare, and they usually don't trade at a discount.

2. Valuation is fair for this growth¶

The bear will say 61.6x trailing earnings, but trailing earnings understate a business growing this fast. On forward earnings the stock trades at 37.5x with a PEG of ~1.5. That's a fair price for a ~35% grower with these margins and this cash profile.

The forward EPS of $5.22 looks like a big jump from the $3.80 annualized run-rate. But the vendor doesn't say which period it covers, and my reading is that it's roughly a next-year number. H1'26 EPS was $1.75, so 2026 likely lands near $3.9 if growth holds. That would make $5.22 about +33% growth, in line with what the company is delivering now. That's my inference, not a confirmed figure, but it doesn't require a heroic assumption.

3. This sell-off looks macro-driven¶

  • The headlines point to rates, not Arista. This week brought a hot CPI print, a global bond selloff, a Nasdaq drop of 1.65%, and weak chip stocks as money rotated into banks. None of the ANET headlines reports a guidance cut, a downgrade, or a regulatory problem. One of them even notes that the 5.65% drop came with "new analyst support."
  • Analysts are leaning in. BNP Paribas raised its target to $290 from $248. That's one data point, but it sits about 48% above the current price.
  • The macro risk is about multiples, not earnings. Prediction markets put a US recession at 8% and an AI-bubble burst in 2026 at 6%. Rate fears hit the multiple, while the earnings stream keeps compounding.
  • The $6.9B deferred revenue balance points to strong revenue visibility. I'd guess it explains much of the rise in current liabilities, though that needs checking in the 10-Q.

4. The technical picture is a test inside an uptrend¶

  • The weekly and monthly SuperTrend are both UP, and price is 20.5% above the 200-day SMA ($162.16).
  • The stock is at the lower Bollinger Band, and the daily z-score of −1.97 is almost at the oversold threshold.
  • It has absorbed bigger drawdowns before, running from a 116.13 close on 3/30 to 216.74 on 10/9, a gain of about 87%.

5. Getting ahead of the bear¶

"Gross margin is compressing." It is, from 65.3% to 62.9%. But gross profit dollars grew 33% YoY, and operating margin still rose to 45.4% from 44.7%. Gross margin also recovered 100 bps from Q1 to Q2. The tools don't give a cause, but a mix shift toward large cloud and AI customers is a plausible one, and a business gaining share at that scale can accept it.

"Insiders sold $1.4B this year." I won't wave this away, because there is no insider buying. But the selling looks systematic. Bechtolsheim, Duda and Giancarlo sell on regular schedules, and the CEO has sold continuously since about $102 in June 2025. If you had treated that selling as a signal, you would have missed a double. The data doesn't say whether these are 10b5-1 plans, so that's a reasonable inference and not a certainty.

"Q2 operating cash flow fell 36% QoQ." Q1 was unusually strong at 166% of net income, so Q2 partly gave that back. The half-year picture (+51% YoY) is what matters, and TTM FCF is still 96% of net income.

"Nvidia is pushing into Ethernet." I can't tell from headlines whether the market sees that as a threat or as validation. What I can see is that Arista grew revenue 38% while this competitive debate was playing out, and nothing in the numbers shows share loss yet.

6. What I'd concede¶

The daily trend is fragile, with the SuperTrend stop only 0.42% below the close. ADX is ~20, so there's no strong trend, and MACD has crossed bearish. There's a 30% market-implied chance of a Fed hike on Oct 29, and the Q3 earnings date (likely early November) is unconfirmed. The bar is high.

So I'm not claiming the bottom is in, and a break below ~$194.60 could extend the drop toward the September lows near $188. My claim is that the fundamental trajectory (accelerating growth, rising margins, huge cash flow) hasn't changed while the price has been pushed around by rates. When those two diverge in a quality compounder, I want to own the fundamentals.
