A simple illustration shows why the rebound should not automatically be treated as an attractive entry:

Using 244.76 only as a hypothetical entry reference:

  • Distance to the latest low, 237.80: 6.96 per share.
  • Distance to the latest high, 250.12: 5.36 per share.
  • Reward to that immediate hurdle versus that risk distance: approximately 0.77:1, before costs.

This is not a proposed trade or stop placement. It illustrates why an execution decision needs either a better entry location, stronger evidence of broader upside, or a different defensible invalidation structure.

A general sizing framework is:

Position size = predetermined monetary risk budget ÷ entry-to-invalidation distance

Allow separately for slippage, gaps, and transaction costs.
