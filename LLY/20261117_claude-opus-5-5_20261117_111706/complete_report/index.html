The thesis¶

LLY is the rare mega-cap that is growing like a small-cap, with 80%+ gross margins and operating cash flow well ahead of earnings. It is entering a rate-hike cycle with a firm economy behind it. The stock is stretched short-term, but the primary trend, the fundamentals and the positioning all favor owning it.

1. Growth at a scale that rarely exists¶

  • Cash: Nine-month operating cash flow was $27.4B, up 102%, or 131% of net income. Q3 operating cash flow of $11.4B was nearly double Q3 net income. These earnings are backed by cash.
  • Diversification: The week's news adds a ~$1.5B Calytrix Bio acquisition, an oncology pact with Stipple Bio worth up to $5.9B, and an Innovation Day focused on the early pipeline. Lilly is building breadth beyond obesity while the core franchise is still compounding.

2. The bear's best card: net income fell two quarters in a row¶

Net income went $7.40B → $7.10B → $6.45B while revenue rose about 23%. I won't dodge this, but I'd frame it differently.

  • Pricing power is not the problem. Gross margin was 83.9% in Q3, so the squeeze is below the gross-profit line. Between gross profit and net income, the gap widened from about $8.8B in Q1 to about $14B in Q3 (my arithmetic). That points to R&D, launch spending, acquired in-process R&D or tax, not a weakening product.
  • The Q2 balance sheet fits that pattern. Assets jumped $25.7B in one quarter and investing outflow was $15.4B, which looks like deal activity. Under US accounting, acquired in-process R&D is often expensed immediately. That is my inference, not something the feed confirms, and the 10-Q will settle it. As an illustration, if sequential growth stays near Q3's 6%, revenue would be about $120B over the next four quarters. At the 37% net margin Lilly printed in Q1'26, that is roughly $44B of net income, or about $50 per share. This is my back-of-envelope math, not a forecast, and it needs margins to recover.
