If the objection is that growth must eventually slow, I agree. But slower growth and deteriorating economics are different propositions. NVDA does not need to double revenue indefinitely for the investment to work.

2. NVDA’s competitive advantage is broader than one accelerator chip¶

The business-model argument is that NVDA competes across GPUs, networking, systems, and software, rather than solely on the price and specifications of an individual semiconductor.

Its established CUDA software ecosystem is also a qualitative source of switching friction: customers must consider development tools, compatibility, integration work, and deployment reliability—not just competing hardware prices. The supplied research does not quantify those switching costs or current market share, so I would not claim an impregnable monopoly.

But the financial results are consistent with meaningful differentiation. More than doubling revenue while expanding gross margin is difficult to reconcile with a claim that NVDA’s economics have already been commoditized. It does not prove competitors cannot catch up; it shows that competitive collapse is not evident in the supplied results.

There are several potential growth avenues:

  • Continued investment in AI training.
  • Deployment and expansion of inference workloads.
  • Networking and systems required to connect accelerated-computing infrastructure.
  • Software and integration that make that infrastructure usable.

Efficiency improvements are a legitimate complication. Less compute per task could reduce hardware requirements, while lower costs could expand usage. The relevant question is whether workload growth exceeds efficiency gains, not whether efficiency automatically destroys demand.

NVDA also has substantial internal scalability: fiscal 2026 reported capital expenditure was approximately 2.8% of revenue. That supports a relatively capital-light internal model, although it does not capture supplier commitments or eliminate manufacturing, memory, packaging, and power constraints.
