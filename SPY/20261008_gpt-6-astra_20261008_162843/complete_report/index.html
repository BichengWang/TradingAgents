7. Risk/reward and data limitations¶

Why chasing the latest close may be unattractive¶

Using the verified close solely as a reference:

  • Distance from 773.93 to the October 6 high of 781.62: $7.69
  • Distance from 773.93 to the reported daily SuperTrend line of 759.50: $14.43

If that recent high were the first objective and the daily line the risk reference, the illustrative reward-to-risk would be only approximately 0.53R, before costs.

This is not an entry recommendation or a forecast that either level will be reached. It shows why entry location matters: bullish direction can coexist with unattractive nearby reward-to-risk.

Position sizing should follow:

[ \text{Shares}=\frac{\text{Chosen dollar risk}}{\text{Entry price}-\text{Chosen stop price}} ]

A SuperTrend close-based invalidation rule is different from a hard intraday stop order. Waiting for a closing signal can permit a larger loss, while gaps and slippage can cause either approach to exceed its theoretical risk.

Source discipline¶

The verification snapshot controls the latest OHLCV and confirms 50-day SMA, RSI, and ATR. ADX, OBV, SuperTrend, TD-9, and Z-score come from their dedicated indicator outputs and are not independently repeated in that snapshot. No discrepancy appeared in the overlapping latest values.

Historical comparisons above use concrete dated prices or indicator readings from the supplied outputs. No backtest, repeated support-bounce validation, or success rate is established.

The multi-timeframe output does not disclose whether its weekly and monthly calculations use completed or still-forming bars. Those readings should be refreshed at the relevant period closes. This report also does not assess macroeconomic news, earnings, options positioning, or the trader’s portfolio constraints.

Bottom line: SPY’s higher-timeframe direction remains constructive, but weak daily ADX and negative OBV confirmation make timing less compelling. The strongest next evidence would be either a well-supported pullback recovery or a breakout accompanied by better participation—not price strength alone.
