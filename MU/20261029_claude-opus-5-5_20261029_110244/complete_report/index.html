Thesis: MU just printed the largest earnings step-change in its history, converted it to cash, and trades at 4.5x forward earnings after a 17% sector-driven pullback inside an intact higher-timeframe uptrend. I'll start with the strongest bear points, since I expect them.

1. The earnings are real, and the market is not paying for them¶

  • FY26 revenue was $133.2B (+256%), and net income was $85.0B (about 10x). Q4 revenue alone (~$54.2B, derived) exceeded all of FY25 ($37.4B).

2. "It's the top of a memory cycle."¶

That is the bear's best argument, and I won't dismiss it. FY18 peaked at 59% gross margin, followed by a 23% revenue drop in FY19 and a $5.8B loss in FY23. Margins are now 81%.

But look at what MU brings into a downturn this time:

  • $38.4B of cash, a 3.3x current ratio, and reported debt/equity of 3.7%.
  • Equity of $138B, up 156% in a year.
  • Self-funded capex. Capex was $30.7B, or 23% of revenue (down from 42% in FY25), and still left $59B of FCF. Past downturns hurt because the company was spending against falling cash flow. This time it can absorb a price reset and keep investing.
  • A buffer on the balance sheet. The Q4 investing outflow (~$42B) was far larger than capex (~$11B), which suggests ~$31B went into investments. That is my inference, not a confirmed figure, but if it holds, liquidity is larger than the cash line shows.

A cycle turn would hurt the stock, but this balance sheet can survive one.

3. "Growth is decelerating: +75%, +74%, then +31%."¶

Sequential growth is slowing, and I concede that. But +31% in a single quarter, on a $41B base, added about $12.8B of revenue. That is more than the entire dollar increase from Q1 to Q2. Gross margin still expanded to ~87%. Growth is slowing from an extraordinary rate, and the business is still accelerating in dollars.

5. "Rates are at 5%, and a hike is priced."¶

It's a real headwind, and CPI re-accelerating (+0.65% m/m in September) is uncomfortable. But rate sensitivity matters most for stocks whose value sits in distant cash flows. MU at 4.5x forward earnings, with a ~5.5% trailing FCF yield (about 12% on the Q4 run-rate), is mostly valued on near-term cash. It is not a 40x multiple that needs a falling discount rate.
