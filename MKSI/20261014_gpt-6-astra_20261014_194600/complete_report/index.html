  • A technically appropriate stop may require a smaller position than usual.
  • A rebound can be substantial without changing the prevailing trend.
  • Gaps and slippage can cause realized losses to exceed a planned stop distance.

  • 1.5 ATR ≈ 18.38 per share

That calculation does not eliminate gap, liquidity or execution risk.

Key caution: Daily stretch and proximity to the September closing reference create rebound risk. A bearish trend does not automatically make the current close an attractive short entry.

B. Countertrend rebound¶

A rebound case would improve if MKSI:

  1. Stops making lower lows near the current reference area.
  2. Reclaims 244.54, followed by 247.56–249.91.
  3. Shows rising RSI and stabilization or improvement in OBV.

Any such move would initially remain countertrend. The 260.38–264.50 area and declining 269.47 SMA would be subsequent checkpoints, not promised destinations.

A long setup also needs a sufficiently tight, structurally valid invalidation point. If the required stop is far below the entry while the next overhead reference is nearby, the setup may remain unattractive despite improving price action.

C. Broader trend repair¶

More durable repair would require sustained recovery above the contemporaneous daily SuperTrend line, improving behavior around the 50-day SMA, and a higher-low structure supported by volume.

Even a daily bullish flip would not automatically override the higher-priority weekly bearish signal.

Bottom line¶

MKSI’s technical evidence remains bearish, but daily stretch makes timing especially important. The most useful next evidence is whether price stabilizes near 231.50–232.20, breaks that area and fails a retest, or reclaims recently lost closing-price levels with improving momentum and volume.
