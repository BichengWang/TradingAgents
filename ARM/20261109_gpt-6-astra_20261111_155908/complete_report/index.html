Practical consequences:

  1. Tight stops are vulnerable to ordinary fluctuations. A stop only a few dollars away may sit well inside routine price movement.

  2. Position sizing matters more than the nominal share price. A lower-priced ARM is not necessarily a lower-risk ARM.

  3. Stops do not guarantee execution at the chosen level. Gaps and slippage can produce losses beyond the planned per-share amount.

  4. Countertrend positions and bearish positions both carry material risk. The broader downtrend creates risk for rebound buyers, while the recent double-digit recovery demonstrates squeeze risk for sellers.

A useful sizing framework is:

Position size = chosen dollar-risk budget ÷ planned per-share risk, with additional allowance for slippage and gap risk.
