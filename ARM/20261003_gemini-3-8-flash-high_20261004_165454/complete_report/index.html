Arm architects, develops, and licenses high-performance, ultra-low-power central processing unit (CPU) cores, neural processing units (NPUs), graphics processing units (GPUs), and interconnected system IP.

Unlike traditional integrated device manufacturers (IDMs) or fabless chipmakers (such as NVIDIA, AMD, or Intel), Arm does not manufacture, package, or sell physical silicon. Instead, its business model operates on a highly profitable dual-revenue engine: 1. Licensing Revenue: Upfront and milestone-based fees paid by semiconductor vendors and OEMs to access Arm architectures (Armv8, Armv9), Compute Subsystems (CSS), and core designs. 2. Royalty Revenue: Long-tailed, high-margin per-chip royalties paid on every single chip shipped globally that incorporates Arm IP.

Structural Secular Tailwinds¶

  • Armv9 Adoption & Royalty Expansion: Armv9 commands roughly 2x the royalty rate of previous-generation Armv8 architectures, driving substantial blended royalty rate expansion across smartphones, client computing, and servers.
  • Hyperscaler Cloud & Custom Silicon: Transition of cloud data centers toward power-efficient custom Arm-based silicon (e.g., AWS Graviton, Google Axion, Microsoft Cobalt, and NVIDIA Grace).
  • Edge AI & Next-Gen Client Computing: AI-capable PCs (Windows on Arm / Copilot+ architectures) and mobile devices demanding on-device NPU/CPU compute density.
  • Automotive & Industrial IoT: Advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) and software-defined vehicle (SDV) cockpit architectures standardizing on Arm functional safety cores.
