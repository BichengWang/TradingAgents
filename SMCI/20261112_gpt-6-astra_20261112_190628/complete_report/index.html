Overall assessment¶

There is an important counterpoint: RSI shows an early positive momentum discrepancy despite a lower closing price. That raises the possibility of a countertrend rebound, but price structure, volume, and exhaustion signals have not confirmed a durable reversal.

The distinction for traders is therefore bearish trend versus entry timing. Evidence supports continued caution, but an established downtrend does not automatically make a fresh short attractive after a sharp decline.

6. Volatility and execution risk¶

Verified ATR is 2.72098, approximately 10.3% of the latest closing price. This is a historical volatility measure, not a prediction that the next session will stay within that range.

Implications include:

  • Tight stops may be triggered by ordinary price fluctuations.
  • Position size should reflect the distance to a defensible invalidation point.
  • The current daily SuperTrend reference is roughly 3.1 ATR above the close, making it a wide risk reference for a hypothetical short entered near the latest price.
  • Overnight gaps can bypass planned exits. The move from the November 11 close of 30.43 to the November 12 open of 27.19 demonstrates that risk directly.

A practical sizing framework is:

Position size = permitted dollar loss ÷ planned per-share risk, with allowance for slippage and gap exposure.

The key reversal warning is the tentative RSI divergence; the principal objections are bearish timeframe alignment, weak participation, and incomplete exhaustion signals.

For decision-making, distinguish continuation below 25.705 from stabilization above 28.995 and 30.43. Neither scenario should be treated as confirmed in advance. This assessment does not incorporate verified company news, fundamentals, borrow availability, or options positioning.
