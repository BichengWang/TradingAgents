6. Volatility and risk: tight stops face substantial noise risk¶

Verified ATR: 2.32, approximately 5.4% of the current closing price.

The latest close-to-close decline of 2.17 was approximately 0.94 ATR. The percentage loss was substantial, but its size relative to ATR means it is not, by itself, proof of an exceptional volatility event.

Two illustrative distances show the risk-management problem:

  • From 42.77 to the latest session low of 41.88: 0.89, or approximately 0.38 ATR.
  • From 42.77 to the reported daily SuperTrend level of 37.34: 5.43, or approximately 2.34 ATR.

A stop immediately beneath the latest low would be relatively tight compared with prevailing volatility. Using the daily SuperTrend level would permit much more movement and require a smaller position for the same dollar-risk budget.

A basic sizing framework is:

Shares = permitted dollar risk ÷ absolute difference between planned entry and stop

Actual losses can exceed this calculation because of gaps, slippage, and transaction costs.

There is also a reward-to-risk constraint. If an analyst used the recent verified closing high of 44.94 as a target reference and 37.34 as an initial stop reference from 42.77, the illustrative reward-to-risk ratio would be only about 0.40:1. Those are not recommended orders; the calculation demonstrates why a distant trailing level should not be adopted automatically without evaluating the upside objective.

The most useful next evidence is the interaction between the fast EMA, the latest session low, and the weekly trend boundary—supported by momentum and volume rather than price alone.
