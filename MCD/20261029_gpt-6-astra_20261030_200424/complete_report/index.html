But deceleration is not contraction. Q2 operating income still increased 3.3%, diluted EPS increased 5.7%, and operating margin remained 47.02%, down approximately 21 basis points year over year.

My argument is not that momentum is accelerating everywhere. It is that MCD is sustaining earnings growth and exceptionally high consolidated profitability despite a difficult consumer environment. That is a stronger starting point than the broad consumer-pressure narrative suggests.

2. The competitive advantage is the business architecture¶

MCD’s advantage is not merely selling familiar food. It combines a global brand and repeatable operating system with franchise royalties, rent, and related fees, alongside company-operated restaurants.

That structure creates several potential growth channels:

  • Higher sales at existing franchised restaurants can expand royalty revenue.
  • Profitable restaurant development can add recurring fees and rent.
  • Brand recognition and operating consistency support a proposition built around convenience and value.

This is more scalable than relying exclusively on directly operating every incremental restaurant. But it is not costless growth: franchisee profitability and MCD’s own capital requirements remain important.

The market opportunity is also less bleak than a blanket “consumers have stopped eating out” argument implies. September restaurant-category sales increased 6.99% year over year, versus 3.46% food-away-from-home inflation. That produces a rough real-spending proxy of 3.4% growth.

That is not MCD traffic data. Nevertheless, it indicates that demand remains available for restaurant brands that execute well on value and convenience.

For an illustrative underwriting case, 4%–6% annual revenue growth would take the supplied $27.702 billion trailing revenue base to approximately $28.8–$29.4 billion over a subsequent twelve-month period. Those are scenario assumptions—not guidance or consensus—and they do not require repeating Q1’s unusually strong growth.

3. Cash flow is a stronger rebuttal than accounting earnings alone¶

The most encouraging financial evidence is that cash generation improved while investment spending increased.

H1 capital expenditure rose 16.8%, yet calculated free cash flow still increased 18.4%. Operating cash flow covered approximately 120% of net income, up from 107% a year earlier.

A bear can reasonably question durability: much of the improvement occurred in Q2, and the extract does not establish how much came from recurring operations versus working-capital timing. Also, full-year 2025 FCF remained slightly below 2023 despite higher earnings.

But the appropriate conclusion is “verify persistence,” not “cash generation is deteriorating.” The latest first-half evidence shows MCD funding higher reinvestment and still producing more cash after capital expenditure.

These are legitimate concerns—but they need context.

  • June cash of $822 million was substantially below the prior year, but $48 million above year-end 2025.
  • The current ratio improved from 0.95× to 1.08× over that six-month period.

  • Negative equity is longstanding in the supplied history, not a newly emerging development.

Negative book equity alone does not establish that the operating franchise is impaired. Equally, strong cash flow does not prove that leverage is safe. Debt maturities, interest coverage, credit facilities, and distribution commitments must be checked before taking an aggressive position.

My bullish position rests on operating cash generation—not on assuming away financing risk.
