4. Risk/reward and trading handoff¶

A bearish trend does not necessarily create an attractive new short entry at the current price.

For illustration only, using:

  • Hypothetical entry: 58.875
  • Reported daily SuperTrend stop: 73.46
  • Downside reference: 52.25, the November 5 close

would create approximately:

  • 14.59 per share of risk
  • 6.63 per share of potential gross reward
  • A reward-to-risk ratio of approximately 0.45:1, before trading costs

That illustrates why trend direction and entry quality must be assessed separately. It is not a proposed order.

For either direction, a basic sizing framework is:

Position size = predefined dollar risk budget ÷ absolute distance between entry and invalidation

The resulting size should also account for gaps, slippage, and—if considering a short—borrow availability and costs, none of which these tools establish.

INTC technical handoff: The evidence favors a bearish trend classification, with meaningful countertrend-rebound risk because weekly price is nearly statistically stretched. Confirmation is more valuable than anticipation here: a downside break needs follow-through, while a recovery needs price reclamation and improving participation.
