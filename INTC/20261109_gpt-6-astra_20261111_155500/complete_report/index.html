That is approximately 10.4% of the latest closing price, calculated from the verified snapshot. ATR measures recent true-range behavior; it is neither a forecast nor a maximum possible daily move.

This has direct implications:

  • Tight stops can sit inside ordinary price noise.
  • Position sizes should reflect the dollar distance to a defensible invalidation level.
  • Nearby upside references may offer limited reward relative to a volatility-aware stop.
  • Stop orders cannot guarantee their execution price through a gap.

For scale, one ATR is approximately 6.20, while 1.5 ATR is approximately 9.29. These are illustrations, not recommended stop placements.

A basic sizing framework is:

Shares = maximum dollar risk ÷ planned entry-to-stop distance

Actual exposure should also account for slippage, gap risk, liquidity, and any portfolio concentration.
