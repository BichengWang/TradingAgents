For comparison, the indicator history reported ATR of 9.18 on October 6. QQQ is now operating with materially wider daily movement.

Two practical consequences follow:

  1. Tight stops are more vulnerable to ordinary price noise.
  2. Position sizes should generally shrink when stop distances widen, if the cash-risk budget is unchanged.

For scale—not as prescribed stop levels:

  • 1.5 ATR: approximately 24.26 points
  • 2 ATR: approximately 32.35 points

A risk-based sizing framework is:

[ \text{Shares}= \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Maximum intended cash loss}} {|\text{Entry price}-\text{Stop price}|} \right\rfloor ]

The stop should reflect the setup’s invalidation point and holding period, not an ATR multiple chosen in isolation. Gaps, slippage, and transaction costs can make realized losses larger than the intended amount.

Importantly, a rising price does not necessarily mean falling risk: the latest ATR remains elevated.
