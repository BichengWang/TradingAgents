Executive assessment¶

ADBE is showing a constructive recovery, but not yet an unambiguous, fully established uptrend. Its latest close is above both its 50-day and 200-day moving averages, RSI favors positive momentum, and weekly and daily SuperTrend readings point upward. However, the moving-average series remain weak in slope, monthly SuperTrend remains bearish, and ADX has not yet cleared the usual strong-trend threshold.

This favors evaluating a confirmed breakout or a controlled retest rather than treating any rise as sufficient confirmation.

1. Indicator selection and suitability¶

Eight indicators were selected to answer distinct questions:

  • close_50_sma: Has ADBE repaired its medium-term trend?
  • close_200_sma: Has price recovered its longer-term benchmark?
  • supertrend: What direction and trailing-stop structure apply across weekly, monthly, and daily timeframes?
  • adx: Is the recovery developing meaningful trend strength, or does it remain vulnerable to range-bound whipsaws?
  • rsi: Is momentum supportive without already reaching conventional overbought territory?
  • atr: How much normal price movement must a stop and position size accommodate?
  • obv: Does volume participation support the recovery?
  • z_score: Is price stretched relative to its own recent distribution across different timeframes?

The two moving averages address different horizons. SuperTrend supplies directional stop structure, while ADX measures strength rather than direction. RSI and z-score are interpreted differently: momentum can remain healthy even when price is statistically stretched.

6. Volatility and trade geometry¶

ADBE’s verified daily ATR is approximately 3.45% of its latest close, indicating substantial normal movement.

Two consequences follow:

  1. Tight stops can be overwhelmed by ordinary volatility. A stop only a small distance below an entry may sit well inside normal daily movement.
  2. Broader stops require smaller positions. A distant model stop is not a reason to tolerate greater account risk.

An illustrative two-ATR risk distance is 18.22 per share, before transaction costs or gap risk. That is a sizing reference, not an automatic stop recommendation.

Why entry location matters¶

Using the latest close solely as an illustration:

  • Distance from 263.82 to the prior close of 270.55: 6.73
  • Distance from 263.82 to the 200-day SMA at 252.24: 11.58
  • Distance from 263.82 to the daily SuperTrend line at 239.82: 24.00

The first upside reference therefore offers approximately 0.58 times the distance to the 200-day average, or 0.28 times the distance to the daily SuperTrend line.

These are reference-level comparisons, not prescribed targets and stops. They do not cap potential upside. They show why an immediate entry requires more justification than “price is above its averages”: the nearby upside reference may offer limited compensation for a broad invalidation level.

Price and momentum have strengthened, but the latest pullback, daily statistical stretch, lagging volume confirmation, and bearish monthly regime argue against treating the setup as an uncomplicated breakout.
