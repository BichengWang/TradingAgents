Bull Researcher¶

Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for ADBE: A Growing, Cash-Generating Franchise at a Discounted Price

I'll start with what the market is pricing in and then show where the data disagrees.

1. The market is pricing a business in decline, but the numbers show one that's accelerating¶

ADBE is about 29% below its October 2025 closing high and trades at roughly 14x trailing GAAP earnings. That is a price for a company whose core is being disrupted. The latest results say otherwise:

  • Revenue growth is accelerating. It went from about 10.5% through FY25 to 12.0%, 12.7% and 12.9% across the last three quarters. Q3 revenue was $6.76B, up 12.9% YoY.
  • Gross margin is still 88.7%. A business that generative AI was commoditizing would show pricing pressure, and this doesn't.
  • A disrupted franchise doesn't speed up. If the "business model was destroyed" thesis from StockTwits were right, growth would be fading by now. It's doing the opposite.

I'll concede that revenue can lag the damage. But the burden of proof has shifted to the bears, and the reported numbers don't support them.

2. The cash flow is the core of the thesis¶

  • TTM free cash flow is about $9.35B on a $99B market cap, a roughly 9.4% FCF yield.
  • Nine-month FCF is up 11% YoY, with a ~38% FCF margin and capex under 1% of revenue.
  • Q3 operating cash flow was up about 14.8% YoY.
  • EV/EBITDA is about 10x, and net debt/EBITDA is only about 0.25x.

Applying an 8% FCF yield, still generous for a business this capital-light, gives about $300 per share. That is an illustration and not a forecast, but it sits below where the stock traded in January 2026. The market doesn't have to love Adobe for the stock to work. It only has to stop pricing a collapse.

3. Buybacks at this price create real value¶

The bear will say EPS growth is "just buybacks." Look at the pace and the price:

  • Diluted shares fell from about 437M to about 396M in six quarters, and about 7% over the last year.
  • Financing outflows ran about $6.8B over nine months, roughly 9% of market cap annualized.
  • Retiring stock at a ~9% FCF yield is a high-return use of cash. Adobe did far more expensive buybacks when the stock was in the $500s and $600s.

Lower prices mean each repurchase dollar retires more shares. The market's pessimism makes the buyback cheaper.
