6. Conditional trading framework¶

A. Bullish continuation¶

Evidence would improve if NOW:

  1. Closes above 159.50 and subsequently holds the breakout.
  2. Maintains a rising OBV slope, ideally exceeding the August comparison high in the same series.
  3. Shows stronger participation rather than relying solely on another low-volume price extension.

Because daily exhaustion signals are already elevated, an intraday breakout alone would be weaker evidence than sustained closing confirmation.

B. Controlled pullback¶

A retracement toward 153.12–153.72 could offer a more informative decision point if:

  • Selling pressure moderates.
  • Price stabilizes and reclaims the area after testing it.
  • RSI remains consistent with positive momentum.
  • OBV avoids a pronounced deterioration.

The key is the reaction at the area, not the assumption that a lower price is automatically a better trade. Invalidation should be based on the actual pullback structure and volatility.

C. Failed breakout or broader deterioration¶

A close below 153.12, particularly with weakening OBV, would weaken the immediate continuation case. The 148.57–149.54 reference area would then become more relevant.

A subsequent loss of the current 138.24 daily SuperTrend line and 137.49 50-day SMA would represent deeper tactical damage. Those levels will change over time and must be refreshed.

The distant weekly stop should not be used to justify turning a failed short-term trade into an unplanned long-term holding.

7. Volatility and risk calibration¶

The verified ATR is 6.12 per share. It measures recent trading-range volatility, not direction or a guaranteed future range.

For scale:

  • A 2-ATR distance is approximately 12.24 per share.
  • The distance from the verified close to the current daily SuperTrend line is 20.72 per share.
  • The distance from the close to the monthly SuperTrend checkpoint is 10.04 per share.

If a trader illustratively used 138.24 as a stop reference and 169.00 as an upside checkpoint from 158.96, the checkpoint-to-risk ratio would be only about 0.48R before costs. That is not a complete trade plan, and 169.00 is not a forecast. It illustrates why a bullish trend can coexist with unattractive immediate entry geometry.

A general sizing framework is:

Position size = dollar risk budget ÷ risk per share, allowing separately for slippage, gaps, and execution costs.

Bottom line¶

NOW’s primary technical direction is constructive, but short-term entry risk is elevated. Trend alignment and recent participation favor the bullish interpretation; daily exhaustion, statistical stretch, and incomplete longer-window momentum confirmation argue against an indiscriminate chase.

The most useful next evidence is either sustained continuation above 159.50 with participation or a constructive pullback that establishes a clearer invalidation point. Earnings, news, valuation, and other event risks were not assessed by these tools.
