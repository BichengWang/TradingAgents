2. NOW’s competitive opportunity is workflow execution—not merely AI features¶

The strongest strategic argument for NOW is its position in enterprise workflow and automation.

Replacing an established workflow platform involves more than finding software that can generate a similar screen or answer a question. Enterprises must consider integrations, permissions, approval processes, operational continuity, and accountability. Those requirements can create switching costs and opportunities to expand across adjacent workflows.

That distinction matters in the AI debate.

Cheaper software creation does not automatically make governed enterprise execution easy. As businesses deploy more AI agents, they still need to determine:

  • What an agent is permitted to do.
  • Which systems it can access.
  • When human approval is required.
  • How actions are recorded and audited.

That is a credible expansion opportunity for an enterprise-workflow platform such as NOW.

But let’s separate the opportunity from proof. The supplied material does not verify the claimed “Arc” announcement, associated partnerships, or incremental AI revenue. Nor does it provide retention metrics that quantify NOW’s switching-cost advantage.

The bullish proposition is that AI could increase the value of enterprise orchestration—not that AI monetization has already been demonstrated.

The central question is therefore specific: Why did delivering the revenue become so much more expensive?

The summaries do not establish whether the answer is infrastructure costs, business mix, acquired operations, amortization, or something else. We should not assume a temporary explanation. Equally, we should not assume permanent impairment without investigating the cost bridge.

Historical scalability does not guarantee recovery. It does, however, rebut the claim that NOW has never demonstrated an ability to scale profitably.
